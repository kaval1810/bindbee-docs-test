This is the round trip behind most benefits products: read the enrolment, calculate the amount, write it to payroll, confirm it landed.

## What you'll use

**Models** - employee, benefit, employer benefit, employee payroll run, payroll run calendar
**Events** - sync completed, data model change

## Constraints specific to this combination

**Plan-to-deduction-code mapping is yours to own.** You can read the codes the customer has defined - [Get Payroll Codes](/hris/payroll-codes/get-payroll-codes) returns each one's `name`, `code`, `type` and `sub_type`. What no model gives you is the link from a benefit plan to the code that represents it in payroll. Codes are defined per customer and don't follow a standard, so establish the mapping with each customer explicitly during onboarding and store it against the connector. A wrong code posts a real deduction against the wrong plan, and it surfaces as a payroll discrepancy weeks later rather than as an API error.

**Plan year cannot be derived from the payroll date.** Payroll runs carry period and check dates; benefits carry coverage and effective dates. A deduction paid in January can belong to the prior plan year because payroll timing and plan year boundaries don't align. Assign the plan year from the benefit's effective dates, never from the payment date.

**The cut-off is invisible through the API.** Payroll closes for processing before the pay date, and that deadline is the customer's operational schedule, not a field you can read. A write submitted after close is either rejected or silently applied to the next period. Agree the deadline per customer and treat missed changes as catch-up adjustments in the following period rather than assuming a retroactive edit is possible.

**Sync latency sits between election and write.** An election made in the HRIS isn't visible to you until the connector syncs, so the usable window between election and payroll cut-off is shorter than it appears. Where timing is tight, [force a resync](/how-to/sync-control/force-a-resync) rather than waiting for the schedule.
