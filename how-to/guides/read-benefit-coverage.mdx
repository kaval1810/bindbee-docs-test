Recommending a better product means knowing what someone already has. Enrolment alone does not tell you that - two employees on the same plan can carry very different coverage amounts and cover very different people.

**Benefit coverage is the model that closes that gap.** It sits below enrolment and records the amount, the currency, whether the employee themselves is covered, and which dependents are.

<Warning>
  Benefit coverage is a **BETA** model. It works, but field names and shapes are not yet frozen. Check [Model availability](/integrations-coverage/model-availability) before promising it for a given integration - coverage for this model is thinner than for enrolment.
</Warning>

## The two models you need

| Model | Answers | Key fields |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Benefit** | What is this employee enrolled in? | `plan_name`, `provider_name`, `benefit_plan_type`, `benefit_plan_category`, `coverage_tier`, `employee_contribution`, `company_contribution` |
| **Benefit coverage** | How much, and who does it cover? | `amount`, `currency`, `is_employee_covered`, `covered_dependents`, `benefit` |

Coverage points back at an enrolment through `benefit`, so the join runs coverage → benefit → employee.

## Steps

<Steps>
  <Step title="Read current enrolments">
    ```bash
    curl -G https://api.bindbee.dev/api/hris/v1/benefits \
      --header 'Authorization: Bearer <BINDBEE_API_KEY>' \
      --header 'X-Connector-Token: <CONNECTOR_TOKEN>' \
      --data-urlencode 'page_size=200'
    ```

    Filter to what is currently in force. `end_date` in the past means the enrolment has lapsed, and `effective_date` can be in the future - a plan selected during open enrolment is not yet active.

    **Result:** The plans this population actually holds today.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Read the coverage behind each enrolment">
    ```bash
    curl -G https://api.bindbee.dev/api/hris/v1/benefit-coverages \
      --header 'Authorization: Bearer <BINDBEE_API_KEY>' \
      --header 'X-Connector-Token: <CONNECTOR_TOKEN>' \
      --data-urlencode 'page_size=200'
    ```

    Group by `benefit` to attach coverage to its enrolment.

    <Note>
      `amount` and `currency` travel together. A population spanning several countries returns several currencies, and comparing the numbers without the currency produces confident nonsense.
    </Note>

    **Result:** Coverage amounts joined to plans.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Establish who is actually covered">
    Two fields, and they are independent. `is_employee_covered` says whether the employee themselves is included - it is not implied by the existence of the enrolment. `covered_dependents` lists who else is.

    Compare that against the dependents on record. **The gap between dependents who exist and dependents who are covered is the recommendation** - a family of four on employee-only coverage is the clearest signal in this dataset.

    **Result:** Covered versus coverable, per employee.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Compare against what you offer">
    With amount, tier and covered dependents in hand, the comparison is yours to make: under-insured relative to salary, dependents uncovered, or a tier that no longer matches household size.

    <Warning>
      `coverage_tier` is an enum, and a source system can return a value outside the documented set. Handle unrecognised values rather than assuming the set is closed - see [Enum values](/explanation/enum-values).
    </Warning>

    **Result:** A ranked list of employees whose current coverage does not fit.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Keeping it current

Coverage changes at open enrolment, on qualifying life events, and whenever a dependent is added. Re-read on `modified_after` rather than refetching the population - see [Querying the data](/how-to/reading-data/filter-and-narrow-a-collection).

## If this didn't work

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="benefit-coverages returns an empty collection">
    Most likely the integration does not support the model. Coverage is thinner here than for enrolment - check [Model availability](/integrations-coverage/model-availability). Then confirm the model is in scope.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Coverage rows do not join to any enrolment">
    Join on `benefit`, not on the employee. A coverage row references its enrolment; going via the employee produces cross-products where someone holds several plans.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Amounts look implausible">
    Check `currency` before comparing. Also check the value is not a per-period contribution being read as a coverage amount - `employee_contribution` and `company_contribution` live on the enrolment, not on the coverage.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Dependents appear covered who should not be">
    `covered_dependents` reflects the source system. Where a dependent has aged out but the record has not been updated, the source is what Bindbee returns - see [Missing or Unexpected Records](/how-to/troubleshooting/trace-a-missing-record).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
