## Constraints specific to this combination

**Every input is a gated field.** Date of birth, home address, and compensation are the three most commonly restricted fields in an HRIS, and all three are required here. They return null rather than erroring, so an allowance calculated on incomplete data produces a plausible wrong number. Verify all three are populated before running any calculation, and treat a null as a blocker rather than a default.

**Home address, not work location, determines the rating area.** Individual market premiums are rated on where the employee lives. The employee model's work location is the wrong field, and it's the one more likely to be populated. Remote employees make this material rather than an edge case.

**Compensation is a shape, not a number.** Employees may be salaried, hourly, or variable, and the affordability test needs an annualised figure. Hourly employees require an hours assumption the HRIS does not supply. Agree the annualisation method with the customer rather than inferring it, and expect the compensation model to require normalisation per integration.

**Age is evaluated at a policy date, not today.** Premium age bands are assessed at a specific point - typically the plan effective date. Calculating from the current date drifts as the year progresses and puts employees near a band boundary on the wrong side of it.

**Scoping is in tension with the calculation.** These are exactly the sensitive fields a customer's security review will ask you to exclude. You cannot exclude them and still calculate. Be explicit with customers that ICHRA affordability requires date of birth, home address, and compensation, and scope out everything else instead - see [Restrict Which Models and Fields Sync](/how-to/data-model/restrict-models-and-fields).
