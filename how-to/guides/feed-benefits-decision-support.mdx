## Constraints specific to this combination

**The plan attributes that drive recommendations are usually not unified.** Deductibles, out-of-pocket maximums, coinsurance, and contribution amounts are the inputs a recommendation actually depends on, and they're frequently absent from the employer benefit model. Check `raw_data` per integration before designing the model - if they aren't there either, the plan detail has to come from the customer or the carrier rather than from the HRIS, which changes the architecture substantially.

**Employer contribution is what makes the comparison meaningful.** A recommendation based on gross premium rather than employee cost after employer contribution will systematically favour the wrong plan. Confirm the contribution split is readable before building comparison logic on it.

**Open enrolment is a load spike against a rate limit.** Decision support is used by a whole workforce in a two-week window. Rate limits are 200 requests per minute per connector token, so a per-employee-per-session read pattern will hit the ceiling for large customers. Pre-load the population and serve from your own store during the session rather than reading through - see [Handle API Errors](/how-to/setup/handle-api-errors).

**Refresh before the session, not during it.** Dependent and compensation changes made during enrolment matter to the recommendation. Force a resync ahead of the window rather than relying on the 24-hour cadence to catch them mid-session.

**Family composition is the input most often wrong.** Decision support depends on who the employee is covering, and dependent records are frequently incomplete or stale - recorded for a prior enrolment, or covered elsewhere. Where the recommendation is sensitive to family size, confirm with the employee in your own flow rather than treating HRIS dependent records as authoritative.
