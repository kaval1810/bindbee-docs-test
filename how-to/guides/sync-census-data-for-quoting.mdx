## What you'll use

**Models** - [employee](/hris/employee/get-employees), [employment](/hris/employments/get-employments), [dependent](/hris/dependents/get-dependents), [location](/hris/locations/get-locations), [company](/hris/companies/get-companies)
**Events** - `connector_synced`

<Info>
  **Before you start**

  - You have the carrier's required field list. It determines what you scope, and scope changes need a resync.
  - You've agreed with the customer which population is being quoted. There is no filter that means "eligible".
</Info>

## Steps

<Steps>
  <Step title="Scope to what a census needs">
    Set scope under **Configure → Scoping** before connecting customers. Exclude national ID and bank info - they are never needed for a quote and expand your compliance surface.

    Keep date of birth, home postcode, and location. Those are rating fields, and excluding them breaks the quote rather than the integration.

    <Warning>
      Scope is organisation-level and shared across environments - see [Restrict what syncs](/explanation/scope-and-permissions). It is not a per-customer setting.
    </Warning>

    **Result:** A field set matched to the carrier's requirement.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Read only after a completed sync">
    A census read mid-sync is quietly short. Gate the extract on `connector_synced` rather than a timer - see [Trigger a job on completion](/explanation/how-syncing-works).

    Record the sync timestamp with the extract. A quote references a specific date, and "which day is this census" becomes unanswerable later otherwise.

    **Result:** A stable dataset with a defensible as-of date.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Assemble the population">
    Carriers price eligible employees. Build that set from statuses explicitly rather than reaching for a single filter.

    ```bash
    curl --request GET \
      --url 'https://api.bindbee.dev/api/hris/v1/employees?employment_status=ACTIVE&page_size=200&expand=work_locations' \
      --header 'Authorization: Bearer <BINDBEE_API_KEY>' \
      --header 'X-Connector-Token: <CONNECTOR_TOKEN>'
    ```

    Fetch each status you've agreed on separately - filters are inclusive only, so there is no way to exclude contingent workers in one request. `ACTIVE_EXTERNAL` is usually out; `LEAVE` is usually in, because those employees retain eligibility.

    Expanding `work_locations` gets the rating geography inline instead of N follow-up calls - see [Reading employees](/explanation/employee-and-org-models). Location is currently **BETA**; since the census depends on it for rating geography, pin the fields you extract and re-check them when the contract settles.

    **Result:** The quoted population, per the agreed definition.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Page the full set">
    Follow the cursor to exhaustion. A census that stops at page one is the failure that looks most like success - see [Pagination](/api-reference/basics/pagination).

    **Result:** Every employee in the population.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Add family composition">
    Read dependent benefits for who is actually covered, and dependents for their dates of birth.

    Counting dependent records rather than coverage inflates the census - see [Sync Dependents and Beneficiaries](/how-to/guides/sync-dependents-and-beneficiaries).

    **Result:** Household composition, priced correctly.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Verify the rating fields are populated">
    Check before delivering, not after. These fields return `null` rather than erroring, so an incomplete census looks complete.

    ```python
    REQUIRED = ["date_of_birth", "employment_status"]
    gaps = {f: sum(1 for e in census if not e.get(f)) for f in REQUIRED}
    # postal_code is nested inside home_location, not a top-level field
    gaps["postal_code"] = sum(1 for e in census if not (e.get("home_location") or {}).get("postal_code"))
    if any(gaps.values()):
        raise CensusIncomplete(gaps)   # a permission gap, not an empty workforce
    ```

    A field null for every record is a permission, not absence - see [Permission errors](/how-to/troubleshooting/resolve-a-permission-error).

    **Result:** A census you can hand to a carrier.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Break the count down by entity">
    ```bash
    curl --request GET \
      --url 'https://api.bindbee.dev/api/hris/v1/companies?page_size=200' \
      --header 'Authorization: Bearer <BINDBEE_API_KEY>' \
      --header 'X-Connector-Token: <CONNECTOR_TOKEN>'
    ```

    Group employees by `company_id` and confirm which legal entities belong in this quote. The credentials may cover more or fewer entities than the customer assumes - see [Record counts](/how-to/troubleshooting/reconcile-record-counts).

    **Result:** A count the customer recognises, per entity.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Constraints specific to this combination

**The census population is a business definition, not a status filter.** Carriers price eligible employees, which usually excludes contingent workers (`ACTIVE_EXTERNAL`) and includes employees on leave who retain eligibility. There is no filter that expresses "eligible" - you assemble it from statuses and agree the definition with the customer. Getting it wrong changes the quote.

**Rating fields are the ones most often gated.** Date of birth, home postcode, and compensation are exactly what carriers need and exactly what sits behind separate sensitive-data permissions. They come back null rather than erroring, so an incomplete census looks like a complete one. Verify these are populated before delivering - see [Permission errors](/how-to/troubleshooting/resolve-a-permission-error).

**Scope narrowly, but not too narrowly.** A census needs fewer models than most integrations, and excluding national ID and bank info is the cleanest answer to a customer's security review. Excluding date of birth or location, however, breaks rating. Decide the field list against the carrier's actual requirements before configuring scope, since changing it later means a resync.

**A census is a point in time.** Quotes reference a specific date. Record the sync timestamp with the extract, and read after a completed sync rather than mid-run - a partial sync produces a census that is quietly short.

**Multi-entity customers need explicit handling.** Where a customer has several legal entities, the connected credentials may cover all, some, or one. Break the count down by `company_id` and confirm which entities are in scope for the quote rather than assuming the connector covers the whole organisation.

## If this didn't work

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="The headcount doesn't match the customer's">
    Population definition, nearly always - contingent workers included, terminated employees retained, or one person holding several employments. See [Record counts](/how-to/troubleshooting/reconcile-record-counts).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="date_of_birth or postcode is null across the board">
    A sensitive-data permission. The census will look complete and price wrong.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The census is short by a few hundred">
    Either pagination stopped early, or it was read mid-sync. Gate on `connector_synced` and follow the cursor to exhaustion.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="A field you need was scoped out">
    Bring it back into scope and resync - the data was never collected, so it cannot be recovered retroactively.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
