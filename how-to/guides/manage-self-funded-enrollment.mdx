## Constraints specific to this combination

**Covered lives, not enrolled employees, is the operative count.** Self-funded plans carry claims risk per person, so the population that matters includes dependents. That count comes from dependent benefits, not from dependents and not from employee enrolments. Counting employees understates exposure; counting dependents overstates it.

**Plan and carrier identifiers usually need mapping.** Administrators and stop-loss carriers identify plans by codes held in the customer's benefits configuration. These are frequently present in `raw_data` but absent from the unified model. Map them as custom fields, and scope per connector - plan codes are customer-specific and an organization-scoped mapping will resolve to nothing elsewhere. See [Scope a Mapping to One Connector or All](/how-to/data-model/scope-a-mapping).

**Retroactive changes are normal here.** Enrolments and terminations are routinely backdated after claims have been processed against the earlier state. An extract that reflects only current state loses that history. Store your own effective-dated record of what you reported and when, rather than reconstructing from the current view.

**Coverage tier is a summary, not a roster.** An employee-plus-family tier tells you the pricing category, not who is covered. Build the roster from dependent benefit records, and expect tier and dependent records to disagree - see [Sync Dependents and Beneficiaries](/how-to/guides/sync-dependents-and-beneficiaries).

**Coverage outlives employment.** Benefit end dates are set independently of termination dates, and continuation coverage extends past both. A population built from active employment status will drop people the plan is still liable for.
