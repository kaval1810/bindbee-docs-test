---
title: "Trace an Unexpected or Duplicate Record"
sidebarTitle: "Unexpected records"
description: "Diagnose records that appear twice, or records you didn't expect at all."
---

Most apparent duplicates aren't duplicates. Work out which of these you have before trying to fix it - the remedies are different and the wrong one makes things worse.

<Info>
  **Before you start**

  - You have the Bindbee `id` of each record involved. Two records with the same `id` is a different problem from two records for the same person.
</Info>

## Steps

<Steps>
  <Step title="Check whether it's two employments, not two employees">
    An employee is a person; an employment is a job. One person legitimately has several employments - a rehire, a transfer, a promotion, or two concurrent roles.

    Systems that terminate-and-recreate on transfer produce several employment records for one continuous tenure. If you're joining employees to employments and rendering the result, one person appears once per employment.

    Fetch employees alone and count. If the duplication disappears, this is your answer - and the fix is selecting the right employment rather than deduplicating people. See [Reading employees](/how-to/reading-data/read-employee-data).

    **Result:** You know whether you have one person or two records.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Compare the identifiers">
    For two employee records that look like the same person, compare `id`, `remote_id`, and `employee_number` across both.

    | Pattern | Meaning |
    | --- | --- |
    | Different `remote_id` | Two distinct records upstream - the duplication is in the source system |
    | Same `remote_id`, different `id` | Identity drift on the Bindbee side - report it |
    | Same `employee_number`, different `remote_id` | Employee numbers aren't unique on this platform; don't key on them |

    **Result:** You know which side the duplication is on.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Check the raw payload">
    Fetch `raw_data` for both records - see [Inspect raw data](/how-to/data-model/inspect-the-raw-upstream-payload).

    Two genuinely distinct upstream records means the source system holds two, and the fix belongs in the customer's HRIS. This is common after a merger, a payroll migration, or a rehire processed as a new person.

    **Result:** Confirmation of what the source actually returns.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Check whether you created it">
    If you write to this connector, a duplicate may be yours - a retried write without a consistent idempotency key creates a second record.

    Check the creation timestamps against your own write logs. See [Employee](/how-to/writing-data-back/create-an-employee).

    **Result:** You know whether the duplicate originated with you.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Records you didn't expect at all

| Symptom | Usual cause |
| --- | --- |
| More people than the customer's headcount | Contingent workers and contractors, commonly `ACTIVE_EXTERNAL` |
| Former employees | Terminated records persist with a terminal status; filter on `employment_status` |
| People from another business unit | The credentials cover more legal entities or companies than expected - filter on `company_id` |
| Records with almost no fields populated | Partially provisioned upstream, or a sync that loaded the employee but not related models |
| Test or system accounts | Present in the source system; filter them out on a stable attribute |

## Preventing the version you cause yourself

**Key on the Bindbee `id`.** Not `remote_id`, not `employee_number`. Some source systems reissue their identifiers on rehire, transfer, or record merge - keying on them creates duplicates in *your* system when the upstream ID changes.

**Send idempotency keys on writes**, derived from your own record identity rather than randomly.

**Don't delete and recreate connectors.** That discards record identities and produces an entirely new set of IDs, which looks like every record was duplicated - see [Rename or delete](/how-to/connections/rename-or-delete-a-connector).

## If this didn't work

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="The same remote_id appears under two Bindbee IDs">
    That shouldn't happen within one connector. Check both records belong to the same connector - IDs are per connector, so the same person across two connectors legitimately has two. If it's one connector, report it with both IDs.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Duplicates appeared after a resync">
    A resync shouldn't create duplicates; it refreshes existing records. If it did, the upstream identifier likely changed during the window, so the record was treated as new. Compare `remote_id` values before and after.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="A rehired employee appears twice">
    Depends on the source system. Some reuse the original record; others create a new one. Where they create a new one, both are genuine upstream records and the customer's HR team owns the merge.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Duplicates only in your system, not in reads">
    Your ingestion is creating them - usually keying on a mutable identifier, or reprocessing without idempotency. A resync makes every record match an incremental filter, which is when this surfaces.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
