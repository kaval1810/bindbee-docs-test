---
title: "Multi-entity setups"
description: "What it means when one customer has several tenants, entities, or connectors under one account."
---

Most documentation assumes one customer means one connector. For a meaningful share of mid-market and enterprise customers, it does not, and a data model built on the one-to-one assumption is expensive to unwind later.

## Why one customer needs several connectors

**Separate legal entities.** A group with several operating companies often runs a separate HR tenant per entity, for legal, tax, or historical reasons.

**Separate countries.** Payroll is jurisdictional. A company operating in three countries commonly runs three payroll systems, and sometimes three different vendors.

**Acquisitions not yet consolidated.** An acquired company keeps its own HR system for a period that is usually longer than anyone planned. During it, one commercial customer is two systems.

**Split HRIS and payroll.** Core HR in one system and payroll in another is a common architecture, not an anomaly. Employee data and pay data arrive through different connectors.

**Several products from one vendor.** A vendor selling distinct regional or functional products means several connectors even though the customer names one supplier.

## Connectors are independent

Each connector authorises separately, syncs on its own schedule, holds its own credentials, and can break on its own. There is no relationship between them at the platform level - Bindbee does not know that two connectors belong to the same commercial customer, because that grouping exists in your system, not in the data.

The practical consequences:

**Your customer record maps to a set of connectors.** Reading employees for a customer means reading across all of them and combining the results.

**Health is per connector.** One broken connector means partial data for that customer, and your monitoring needs to express that. A customer with three connectors of which one has been broken for a month looks fine at a glance and is missing a third of their people.

**Coverage is the intersection.** If one entity is on a system that does not expose benefits, that capability is absent for those employees regardless of what the other connectors support.

## People appear more than once

The same human employed by two entities in a group, or present in both an HRIS and a payroll system, exists as separate records under separate connectors with different identifiers.

Bindbee will not merge them - identity is resolved within a connector and nowhere else. Deduplicating across connectors is your application's job, and it needs a matching strategy that does not rely on the source identifier, since the systems assign their own.

<Warning>
Headcount and any billing derived from record counts must deduplicate across connectors. Counting records across a multi-entity customer overstates their size, sometimes substantially.
</Warning>

## Why it works this way

Bindbee could offer a customer-level grouping above the connector, and deliberately does not.

The reason is that the grouping is a commercial fact rather than a data one, and only you know it. Whether two tenants are one customer or two depends on your contract, your billing, and how you have chosen to model the relationship - and different products in the same position answer it differently. A group with three subsidiaries might be one account or three, and the platform has no basis for guessing.

Keeping connectors independent means the grouping lives where the knowledge is. It also keeps failure isolated: one entity's credentials expiring does not affect the others, and the customer can fix them separately rather than re-authorising everything.

The cost is that you own the fan-out - reading across connectors, aggregating health, and deduplicating people. That work is unavoidable given the grouping is yours to define; what the platform can do is make each connector behave predictably in isolation.

## What this means for you

- **Model customer-to-connectors as one-to-many from the start.** Retrofitting is expensive.
- **Aggregate connector health per customer.** A single broken connector is partial data, not total failure.
- **Deduplicate people across connectors** using stable attributes rather than source identifiers.
- **Do not derive headcount or billing from raw record counts** for multi-entity customers.
- **Ask about entities and countries during onboarding.** It changes the shape of the implementation.
