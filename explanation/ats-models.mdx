Recruiting data is a process rather than a state, and the models reflect that. The central distinction - and the one most likely to be got wrong - is **between a person and their pursuit of a particular job.**

## Candidate and application are not the same thing

**Candidate** is the person: name, contact details, locations. They exist once, however many roles they pursue, and carry an `applications` array.

**Application** is one candidate's pursuit of one job. It holds `current_stage`, `source`, `applied_at`, `rejected_at` and `reject_reason`.

<Warning>
Status lives on the application, not the candidate. **There is no such thing as a rejected candidate** - there is a candidate whose application to one job was rejected, who may be active on another. Reading rejection at the person level mis-states your pipeline and can wrongly exclude someone still in process.
</Warning>

This is also why counting candidates and counting applications give different numbers, and why the ratio between them is itself informative.

## Two candidate flags worth respecting

`is_private` marks a candidate restricted within the customer's ATS - typically a confidential search. `can_email` records whether they may be contacted.

Neither is enforced by the API. **Both are the customer's stated intent, and both are yours to honour** - surfacing a private candidate or emailing someone flagged otherwise is a problem your product creates, not one Bindbee prevents.

## The job side

**Job** is the opening, with `status` of `OPEN`, `CLOSED`, `DRAFT`, `ARCHIVED`, `PENDING` or `-`, plus `departments`, `offices`, `hiring_managers` and `recruiters`. Note `confidential` here too.

**Job interview stage** is a step in a job's process. Stages belong to jobs rather than being global, because different roles run different processes - an engineering pipeline with a technical screen is not a sales pipeline.

**Department** and **office** are the job's organisational and physical placements. These are ATS-side records and **do not automatically correspond to the HRIS group and location models**, even for the same company. The two systems are configured independently.

## The offer

**Offer** attaches to the **application**, not to the candidate - the same principle again. Someone can hold an offer for one role while still in process on another.

Its `status` runs `DRAFT`, `APPROVAL-SENT`, `APPROVED`, `SENT`, `SENT-MANUALLY`, `OPENED`, `DENIED`, `SIGNED` and `DEPRECATED`, alongside `sent_at`, `closed_at` and `start_date`.

<Warning>
`SENT` is not acceptance and `SIGNED` is not a start. Only `start_date` says when the person actually begins, and it is set well before they do - so an offer pipeline keyed on status alone will report starts that have not happened.
</Warning>

## The process side

| Model | Holds |
| --- | --- |
| **Scheduled interview** | A specific interview event, with time and participants |
| **Scorecard** | Feedback on an application, with `interviewer` and `overall_recommendation` |
| **Activity** | The audit trail: notes, emails, status changes. Coverage varies widely |
| **Reject reason** | The customer's own vocabulary for why an application ended |
| **Remote user** | Staff inside the ATS - recruiter, hiring manager, interviewer |
| **Attachment** | A file, usually a resume or cover letter |
| **Screening question** | Application-form questions and answers |
| **Tag** | A free-form label, highly customer-specific |

Like payroll codes, **reject reasons are locally defined** and carry no portable meaning across customers.

Remote users are staff, not candidates, and are distinct from HRIS employees even when they are the same human.

## EEOC is separated deliberately

**EEOC** holds `race`, `gender`, `veteran_status` and `disability_status` against a candidate, collected for compliance reporting. It is a separate model because the data is sensitive, legally restricted in how it may be used, and in most jurisdictions must not influence hiring decisions.

Keeping it separate makes excluding it from your scope a single decision. **Most applications should exclude it.**

## ATS is the write-heavy category

Seventeen ATS endpoints accept creates, against four on HRIS - more than four times the write surface.

Recruiting systems are built to receive data from job boards, sourcing tools and assessment platforms, so their write APIs are correspondingly open. If you are pushing candidates or applications into a customer's ATS, the surface is likely to support it. See [Write support](/explanation/write-support) for the caveats.

## Why it works this way

**Separating candidate from application is what makes recruiting data queryable at all.**

Folding them together would mean a person record per application, so a candidate applying to three roles becomes three people.

Deduplicating afterwards is guesswork, and the questions recruiting teams actually ask - has this person applied before, how many roles are they in, what happened last time - become unanswerable.

Interview stages belonging to jobs rather than being global is the same principle applied to process. A global stage list would force every role through one pipeline, which no ATS does.

**Isolating EEOC reflects a legal reality rather than a modelling preference.** A model that mixed demographic data into the candidate record would make it arrive by default in every read. Separation makes not receiving it the easy choice.

## What this means for you

- **Read status from applications, never from candidates.**
- **Expect multiple applications per candidate.** Deduplicate on the candidate, aggregate on applications.
- **Honour `is_private` and `can_email`.** The API will not enforce them for you.
- **Do not map ATS departments and offices to HRIS groups and locations** without confirming the customer maintains them consistently.
- **Exclude EEOC from scope** unless you have a specific, lawful reason to hold it.
- **Treat reject reasons as customer-local vocabulary**, not a standard set.
