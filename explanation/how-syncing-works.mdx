Bindbee does not proxy your requests to the source system. When you call `GET /hris/employees`, you are reading Bindbee's copy of your customer's data, assembled by a background process that runs on its own schedule.

Almost everything that surprises people about freshness, latency, and timing follows from that one fact.

## The initial sync

{/* DIAGRAM (tier 2): timeline from authorisation onward - initial sync (full read of every in-scope model, duration varies by tenant size), then the 24-hour recurring schedule, with on-demand resyncs sitting outside the schedule. Mark the window where reads succeed but return partial data, since that is the failure this page exists to prevent. */}

A connector becomes usable the moment your customer authorises it, but it has no data yet. Bindbee immediately begins an initial sync: a full read of every model in your scope, paginated through the source system's API or assembled from a delivered file.

How long this takes depends on the integration and the size of the customer. A small BambooHR tenant may finish in under a minute. A large Workday tenant with several models enabled can take considerably longer.

<Warning>
Reads issued before the initial sync completes return successfully with partial or empty results. There is no error and no distinguishing status on the response body - an employee list that is still filling looks exactly like a customer with no employees.
</Warning>

The reliable signal is the webhook. Sync-status events carry a flag indicating whether the completed sync was the initial one, which is what you should gate first-run logic on rather than polling until the count stops changing.

## Incremental syncs after that

Once the initial sync completes, the connector moves to a recurring schedule. By default this runs once every 24 hours, and the frequency is adjustable.

{/* VERIFY: is sync frequency self-serve in the dashboard, or does it require a support request? Open item from the How-to spec. */}

Subsequent syncs are incremental where the source system supports it - Bindbee asks for records changed since the last run rather than re-reading everything. Where the source system has no reliable change marker, the sync re-reads and reconciles against what it already holds.

You can also trigger a resync on demand rather than waiting for the schedule. This is the right instrument after a customer fixes a permission problem or corrects data upstream and wants to see it immediately.

## Two different timestamps

Every record carries a `modified_at`. It is easy to assume this reflects when the record changed in the HRIS. It does not.

`modified_at` is set by Bindbee when it persisted the record. It moves when a sync writes a change, which means it also moves for reasons unrelated to the source system - a re-sync that reconciles a field, for instance.

The source system's own timestamp, where it provides one, survives inside `raw_data` under whatever name that system uses.

This matters when you filter. Filtering on `modified_at` gives you "records Bindbee has touched since X", which is the right question for keeping a local copy in step. It is the wrong question if you need "records the customer edited since X" - for that you have to read the source timestamp out of `raw_data`, and its availability and name vary by integration.

## Why it works this way

Storing a copy rather than proxying is the decision everything else hangs off, and the trade is freshness for reliability.

A proxying design would return data that is always current, but it inherits every property of the underlying system: its rate limits, its latency, its outages, and its pagination semantics. Your read of an employee list would fail whenever the customer's HRIS was slow, and a page of a hundred employees would cost a hundred upstream calls. Rate limits alone make this impractical - most HR systems allow far fewer requests per hour than a busy application makes.

Holding a synced copy means your reads are fast, consistent, and unaffected by the source system being down. The price is a staleness window, and a sync process whose state you sometimes need to reason about. The 24-hour default reflects what the majority of source systems tolerate without exhausting their rate limits; it is a ceiling imposed by them, not a target chosen by Bindbee.

## What this means for you

- **Gate first-run logic on the sync-status webhook**, not on data appearing. An empty list during the initial sync is indistinguishable from an empty tenant.
- **Treat `modified_at` as "when Bindbee saw it"**, and reach into `raw_data` when you need "when the customer changed it".
- **Trigger a resync after a customer fixes something upstream** rather than telling them to wait until tomorrow.
- **Design for a staleness window.** Data can be up to a full sync interval behind the source system, and no configuration removes that entirely.
