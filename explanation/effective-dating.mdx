---
title: "Effective dating"
description: "Why a change made in the source system is not visible yet, and how future-dated records behave."
---

Your customer says they entered the promotion. You read the employee and the old title comes back. Nothing has failed, and waiting for the next sync will not help.

The change is there. It has not started yet.

## Two different dates

HR systems separate when a change was recorded from when it takes effect, and both are real.

A raise entered on 1 March and effective 1 April is recorded in March and current from April. For the whole of March, the recorded state and the effective state disagree - and the effective state is the one that describes what the employee is paid today.

This is not an edge case. Compensation changes, promotions, transfers, benefit enrolments, and terminations are all routinely entered in advance, because that is how HR administration works. Entering them on the day would be the exception.

## Future-dated records are present in your data

Bindbee syncs what the source system holds, which includes records whose effective date has not arrived. They are ordinary records with dates in the future.

<Warning>
Reading the most recently created compensation or employment record does not give you the current one. It frequently gives you a future one. Anything that surfaces pay or title to a user must select by effective date, not by recency.
</Warning>

The correct selection is the record whose effective period contains today: effective start on or before today, and either no end or an end after today. That is more work than taking the latest record, and taking the latest record is the most common way to show someone a salary they are not yet earning.

Terminations behave the same way. An employee with a future termination date is currently employed. Treating any termination date as past tense will offboard people who are still working.

## Sync timing is a separate problem

Two different delays produce the same symptom, and distinguishing them is the first diagnostic step.

**Sync lag** is Bindbee not having read the change yet. The record does not exist in your data at all. It resolves on the next sync, or immediately on a forced resync.

**Effective dating** is Bindbee having the change and the change not applying yet. The record exists, with a future date. No amount of resyncing changes it.

If the customer swears they made the change and you cannot see it anywhere, that is sync lag. If you can see a record with a date in the future, that is effective dating, and the answer is that everything is working correctly.

## Where the future is legitimate

Some models are forward-looking by design and should be read that way. Payroll run calendars describe periods that have not happened. Benefit plan years often open before they begin. Scheduled interviews sit in the future by definition.

For these, filtering out future records discards the data's purpose.

## Why it works this way

Bindbee could resolve effective dating for you - return only records currently in force and hide the rest. It does not, and the reason is that "currently" is not a stable enough idea to bake into an API.

Applications need different answers at different moments. Payroll reconciliation asks what was in force during a past period. Benefits eligibility asks about a specific date that may be neither today nor now. Onboarding automation asks what will be true when someone starts. An API that pre-resolved to today would serve the simplest case and make the others impossible, since the discarded records would be gone.

Returning the full dated set means every question is answerable, at the cost of requiring you to state which date you mean. That cost is real, and it is why selecting by effective period is worth writing once as a shared helper rather than repeating at each call site.

## What this means for you

- **Select by effective date, never by recency.** The latest record is often the future one.
- **A future termination date means currently employed.**
- **Distinguish sync lag from effective dating** before investigating. They have different causes and only one has a fix.
- **Write the effective-period selection once** and reuse it. Every model with dates needs the same logic.
- **Do not filter future records blindly.** Calendars and plan years are supposed to be ahead.
