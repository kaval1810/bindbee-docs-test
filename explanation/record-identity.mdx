An employee you have tracked for months suddenly appears twice - same name, different identifiers.

Nothing failed. It follows from **how Bindbee decides that two things it has seen are the same thing**, and from one decision it deliberately does not make.

## Two identifiers

| | `id` | `remote_id` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Whose | Bindbee's | The source system's |
| Stable | **Yes** | No - can be reassigned upstream |
| Use it as | Your foreign key | A trace back to the source |

Source systems reassign their own identifiers more often than you would expect: on a rehire, a record merge, a migration, a payroll provider switch. **Store `id`**, and an upstream change costs you nothing.

`employee_number` is not a third identifier. It is an attribute you can filter on - the number shown in the customer's HRIS UI - and it is **not unique on every system**. Use it to find a record someone described to you, never as a key.

## Identity is resolved per connector

Bindbee treats a record as one it already holds when the `remote_id` matches **within that connector**. A new `remote_id` is a new record, always.

There is no name matching, no fuzzy comparison, no attempt to recognise that two identifiers describe the same human. So the same person under two connectors is two records with two different `id` values, and a source system that reissues an identifier produces a second record beside the first.

## Why Bindbee will not merge them

Automatic merging is the obvious feature to want here, and the one thing a platform in this position should not do.

**Merging asserts that two humans are the same human, on evidence that is often ambiguous.** Names repeat. Personal emails get reused. Contractors are rehired as employees with deliberately separate records, because the business needs them separated.

A platform that merges on similarity will eventually merge two different people - one person's compensation, benefits or bank details surfacing under another's identity. **Far worse than a duplicate, and silent.**

Following the source system's identity exactly means Bindbee is never the thing that introduced the error. If the HRIS says these are two records, you get two records - and you keep the ability to decide otherwise using context Bindbee does not have: your own user accounts, your onboarding history, your tolerance for a false match.

The cost is that reconciliation is yours. Match on stable personal attributes, where work email is usually the strongest single signal.

## What this means for you

- **Store `id`, not `remote_id`.** Bindbee's identifier survives upstream changes; the source system's does not.
- **Never key on `employee_number`.** It is a filter, and it is not unique everywhere.
- **Expect reconciliation to be your job**, and build it in early. Retrofitting after duplicates accumulate is materially harder.
- **Treat an unexplained jump in record count as a duplicate signal**, not as growth.
