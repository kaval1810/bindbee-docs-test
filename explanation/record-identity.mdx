Your employee count went up by forty overnight and nobody was hired. Or an employee you have tracked for months suddenly appears twice, same name, different identifiers.

Neither is a bug in the usual sense. Both follow from **how Bindbee decides that two things it has seen are the same thing.**

## Three identifiers

| Identifier | What it is | Stable | Use it for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `id` | Bindbee's UUID | **Yes** - survives upstream change | Your foreign key |
| `remote_id` | The source system's API ID | No - can be reassigned | Tracing a record back to the source |
| `employee_number` | What the customer sees in their HRIS UI | No - **not unique on every system** | Finding a record a human described |

The third is worth knowing because **it is the identifier your customer will quote at you** - "employee 1042 is missing" means the number on their screen.

It is also the one you can least rely on. The UI value is often not what the API returns, so confirm it against `raw_data` before trusting a match.

Use it to locate a record when a person describes one. Never use it as a key.

## Identity is resolved per connector

Bindbee treats a record as one it already holds when the `remote_id` matches **within that connector**. A new `remote_id` is a new record, always.

There is no name matching, no fuzzy comparison, no attempt to recognise that two identifiers describe the same human.

<Info>
The same person appearing under two connectors is therefore two records with two different `id` values. A customer running separate HRIS tenants for two legal entities will have shared employees duplicated across them, and reconciling is your application's job.
</Info>

## Why duplicates appear

Three situations produce a second record for someone who already exists.

**The upstream identifier changed.** Some HR systems let an employee ID be edited, or reassign identifiers during a migration or payroll provider switch. From Bindbee's side that is indistinguishable from a new person arriving while the old one goes quiet.

**The employee was recreated upstream.** A rehire entered as a new record rather than a reactivation, or a record deleted and re-added to fix an error, produces a new source identifier.

**A transfer created a new employment.** Several systems move someone between positions or entities by terminating one record and creating another rather than editing in place.

The common thread: **the source system changed its own notion of identity, and Bindbee followed it.**

## Reconciling when identifiers change

Because Bindbee will not merge records for you, reconciliation happens in your application - and it has to use something other than the identifier that changed.

Match on stable personal attributes: work email, date of birth, full name, or a national identifier where you hold one and may use it.

**Email is usually the strongest single signal** in an HR context. Names alone are not sufficient at any meaningful scale.

Two habits make this much easier:

- **Store Bindbee's `id` as your foreign key**, so an upstream identifier change does not break your own references.
- **Treat a sudden jump in record count as a duplicate signal**, not as growth. A headcount that moves without a hiring event is the clearest early warning you get.

## Why it works this way

Automatic merging is the obvious feature to want here, and the one thing a platform in this position should not do.

**Merging asserts that two humans are the same human, on evidence that is often ambiguous.** Names repeat. Personal emails get reused. Contractors are rehired as employees with deliberately separate records, because the business needs them separated.

A platform that merges on similarity will eventually merge two different people - one person's compensation, benefits or bank details surfacing under another's identity. **Far worse than a duplicate, and silent.**

Following the source system's identity exactly means Bindbee is never the thing that introduced an error. If the HRIS says these are two records, you get two records.

You keep the ability to decide otherwise using context Bindbee does not have: your own user accounts, your onboarding history, your tolerance for a false match.

## What this means for you

- **Store `id`, not `remote_id`.** Bindbee's identifier survives upstream changes; the source system's does not.
- **Never key on `employee_number`.** It is not unique on every system, and the UI value often differs from the API value.
- **Build reconciliation into your data layer from the start**, keyed on work email where available. Retrofitting after duplicates accumulate is materially harder.
- **Treat unexplained count changes as a duplicate signal**, not as growth.
- **The same person under two connectors is two records.** For customers running multiple tenants, that is normal, not an error.
