Bindbee identifies a record against a set of conditions, and creates a new one only when something in that set differs. **Report a duplicate that survives that and Bindbee resolves it.**

Three checks come first, since they account for most reports.

<Info>
  **Before you start**

  - You have the Bindbee `id` of each record involved. Two records with the same `id` is a different problem from two records for the same person.
</Info>

## Steps

<Steps>
  <Step title="Check whether it's two employments">
    An employee is a person; an employment is a job. One person legitimately has several employments: a rehire, a transfer, a promotion, or two concurrent roles.

    Fetch employees alone and count. If the duplication disappears, the fix is selecting the right employment. See [Reading employees](/guides/data-models/employee-data).

    **Result:** You know whether you have one person or two records.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Check whether the source system holds two">
    Compare `remote_id` across both records, then fetch `raw_data` - see [Inspect raw data](/guides/reading-writing/raw-data).

    Different `remote_id` values mean two distinct records exist upstream, putting the fix in the customer's HRIS. Mergers, payroll migrations, and rehires processed as a new person all produce this.

    **Result:** You know which side the duplication is on.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Check whether you created it">
    If you write to this connector, a duplicate may be yours: a retried write without a consistent idempotency key creates a second record.

    Check the creation timestamps against your own write logs.

    **Result:** You know whether the duplicate originated with you.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Report anything left to Bindbee">
    Two records sharing a `remote_id` under one connector mean the unique-record conditions didn't hold, which is ours to fix. Send Bindbee both `id` values and the connector ID; deduplicating on your side would mask it.

    **Result:** Bindbee resolves the duplicate at source.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Records you didn't expect at all

| Symptom | Cause |
| --- | --- |
| More people than the customer's headcount | Contingent workers and contractors, generally `ACTIVE_EXTERNAL` |
| Former employees | Terminated records persist with a terminal status; filter on `employment_status` |
| People from another business unit | The credentials cover more legal entities than expected; filter on `company_id` |
| Records with almost no fields populated | Partially provisioned upstream, or a sync that loaded the employee but not related models |
| Test or system accounts | Present in the source system; filter them out on a stable attribute |

## Duplicates you cause yourself

**Key on the Bindbee `id`.** Source systems reissue `remote_id` and `employee_number` on rehire, transfer, or record merge, so keying on either creates duplicates in *your* system the moment the upstream ID changes.

**Send `X-Idempotency-Key` on writes** - see [Idempotency](/guides/reading-writing/writing-data/idempotency).

**Keep the connector.** Deleting and recreating discards record identities and returns the whole dataset under new IDs, which reads as every record duplicating - see [Relinking versus deleting](/guides/troubleshooting/connector-relink#relinking-versus-deleting).

## Frequently Asked Questions

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="The same remote_id appears under two Bindbee IDs">
    Check both records belong to the same connector first. IDs are per connector, so the same person across two connectors legitimately has two. Within one connector, report it with both IDs.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Duplicates appeared after a resync">
    A resync refreshes existing records. Where one appeared anyway, the upstream identifier changed during the window and the record arrived as new. Compare `remote_id` values before and after.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="A rehired employee appears twice">
    Depends on the source system. Some reuse the original record; others create a new one, in which case both are genuine upstream records and the customer's HR team owns the merge.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Duplicates only in your system, not in reads">
    Your ingestion is creating them: keying on a mutable identifier, or reprocessing without idempotency.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
