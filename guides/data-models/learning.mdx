Learning data answers two questions: what training exists, and who has done it. Seven models cover both, and **the split between assignment and achievement is the one that matters.**

## Assignment and achievement are separate

**Enrollment** is an assignment - someone has been given a course, or started one. It says nothing about whether they finished.

**Completion** is an achievement - someone finished, with `completed_at`, `time_duration`, and where the system records them, `score` and `grade` (`A` to `F`, `PASS`, `FAIL`, or `-`).

A completion references a `course` **and** a `content`, so finishing one module inside a course is a completion in its own right. Counting completions per learner without checking which of the two is set will overstate how many courses they finished.

Reading enrolments and treating them as training records is the most common error here, and in a compliance context a consequential one. **An enrolment means the training was assigned, which is exactly what someone out of compliance also has.**

<Warning>
Enrollment carries a `completion` reference, so **outstanding training is a null check**, not a matching exercise: an enrolment whose `completion` is empty is training assigned and not finished. If your product reports compliance, that gap is the report - not the enrolment count.
</Warning>

## The catalogue

| Model | Holds |
| --- | --- |
| **Course** | The unit of learning people are assigned and complete |
| **Content** | Material inside courses - modules, videos, documents |
| **Category** | Groups courses, using the customer's own taxonomy |
| **Skill** | A competency, associated with courses that develop or assess it |

Content and course reference each other in both directions - a course has `contents`, a content item has `courses` - so the relationship is many-to-many. One video used across three courses is one record, not three.

Coverage thins out towards the bottom of that list. Where a system does not distinguish content from courses, content may be sparse or absent, and **many LMS platforms have no skill concept at all** - the model will be empty for those integrations.

<Warning>
Course, content, category, skill and user all carry a `status` of `ACTIVE`, `PENDING` or `INACTIVE`. **An unfiltered catalogue read includes retired and unpublished courses**, and an unfiltered user read includes deactivated learners - so a compliance denominator built without filtering counts people and courses that are no longer live.
</Warning>

## LMS users are not HRIS employees

**User** is a learner in the LMS, and this is a separate identity from the HRIS employee even when it is the same person.

The systems are provisioned independently and the identifiers do not correspond. Joining learning data to employee data means matching on a stable attribute, and `email_address` on the user is usually the only reliable one.

**Accept that some learners will not match.** Contractors, former employees and external participants often have LMS accounts and no HRIS record. That is worth designing for explicitly rather than treating unmatched users as errors.

## Read-only

No LMS model accepts writes. Enrolling someone in a course, recording a completion, or creating a course are not operations the unified API performs - the only write in the category is deleting a connector.

Where a customer needs them, [passthrough](/guides/extending/passthrough) is the route, with the usual cost of integration-specific code.

## Why it works this way

Keeping enrolment and completion separate follows the domain rather than the convenience of a single model.

Collapsing them into one record with a status field works until you need the interesting cases: a learner who enrolled twice, a completion for a course later removed from the catalogue, an assignment with a due date and no progress.

**Recurring compliance training is the clearest example.** The same course is completed annually, and each completion is a distinct, separately-dated fact. A single record with a status can only hold the most recent one.

Read-only reflects source system reality rather than a deliberate limitation. LMS platforms overwhelmingly treat enrolment as something their own administrators or automation rules perform, and expose narrow write APIs or none.

Read coverage is the category's mature capability; **write coverage largely does not exist upstream to be unified.**

## What this means for you

- **Report on completions, not enrolments.** They answer different questions.
- **Find outstanding training by checking `completion` on the enrolment.** No join required.
- **Match learners to employees on email**, and design for the unmatched case.
- **Filter on `status`.** Catalogue and user reads include `INACTIVE` and `PENDING` records by default.
- **Expect skills and content to be thin or absent** on several integrations.
- **Plan for read-only.** Any write need goes through passthrough.
