---
title: "2. Scope the data you need"
description: "Choose which models and fields Bindbee requests from your customers' systems, and understand why that decision shapes everything downstream."
---

Scope is the list of models and fields Bindbee will request from a connected system. You set it once for your organization and it applies to every connector you create afterwards.

**By the end of this lesson** you will have the HRIS Employee model scoped to the fields your product actually reads. About 10 minutes.

## Context

Doing this before connecting anything is deliberate. Three things follow from scope:

- **What your customer must authorize.** Every model in scope maps to a permission in their source system. Asking for compensation data you never use means asking their HR admin to grant access to salaries.
- **What arrives in your API responses.** A field outside scope is absent from responses. Not `null` with an explanation — simply not there.
- **What syncs cost in time.** Fewer models means faster syncs and fewer moving parts when something goes wrong.

<Info>
  Scope is **shared across environments**. What you configure here in
  Development applies in Production too. It is one of the few settings that
  crosses the environment boundary.
</Info>

## Step 1 — List the fields your product actually reads

Before touching the Dashboard, write them down. Not the ones that might be useful later — the ones a feature would break without.

For this tutorial, use:

- `first_name`, `last_name`
- `work_email`
- `employment_status`
- `department`

Enough to prove the pipeline works. You can widen scope later without re-linking connectors.

**Result:** a short, concrete list to configure against.

## Step 2 — Open the scoping configuration

In the Dashboard, with the environment set to **Development**, go to **Configure → Scoping** and select the **HRIS** category.

**Result:** the list of HRIS models, each expandable to its fields.

## Step 3 — Enable the Employee model and its fields

Enable **Employee**, then within it enable only the five fields from step 1. Save.

**Result:** Employee is in scope with five fields; every other HRIS model is out.

## Step 4 — Review what you excluded

Look at what you left disabled. Two exclusions are worth being deliberate about:

| Excluded | Consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Compensation | No salary data, and your customer never has to grant payroll access |
| Bank info | No payment details, which is what you want unless you move money |

**Result:** you know what will be absent from responses, and why.

## What you just did

You narrowed what Bindbee asks for, which narrows what your customers have to grant.

This also gives you the first of three distinct reasons a field can be missing later. Keeping them separate is most of what makes missing-data problems quick to diagnose:

| Cause | Where it is solved |
| --- | --- |
| Out of scope | This screen |
| In scope, but the source system did not grant permission | [Go-live checklist](/how-to/go-live-checklist) |
| Not carried by the unified model at all | [Lesson 6](/get-started/tutorials/add-a-custom-field) |

Permission failures are the quiet one — the field is omitted rather than erroring.
