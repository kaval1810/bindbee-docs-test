What the three have in common

None of them has an Explanation tab, a How-To tab, or a Reference tab. Merge and Finch group by subject; Kombo groups by product and then repeats the same six sections inside each of its four APIs. v2 was the only documentation set of the five organised by document type — and the only one where a reader had to know what kind of page they wanted before they could find the subject.

What moved, and why

Explanation tab dissolved

All 25 pages redistributed — 3 to Get Started, 22 into the topic they explain. A reader looking for webhooks no longer has to know whether they want the concept, the procedure or the reference.

Tutorial → Quickstart

An overview plus seven numbered lessons became one page. The shortest path to a first 200 was four pages and ~2,750 words; scoping, custom fields and webhooks were never needed for it and already had their own guides.

Use cases surfaced

The six end-to-end guides sat in group 10 of 10, inside the third tab. They are the pages an evaluator most needs, and they are now one click from the landing page.

Webhooks consolidated

Eight pages across three tabs became four in one group, 5,099 words to 3,687. Finch answers the same subject in one page of ~2,425 words; Merge in one, Kombo in two.

Platform tour merged

Eight pages averaging 223 words each — never eight subjects, one walkthrough cut into stubs. Now a single Platform page whose sections link into the topic guides.

Two pages named “Webhooks”

The only duplicate title in the repo, and the reason “which webhooks page do I open” had no answer. Resolved, along with 20 stale link labels pointing at renamed pages.

Reference kept clean

API Reference now holds the three primitives, the slug tables, and 136 generated endpoint pages driven by spec.json. No generated page is ever patched by hand.

Proposed: three tabs, split by how each part grows

Not v3 rearranged. The Guides tab follows the three questions a developer asks in order. The other two tabs are catalogs, separated because they grow mechanically — one with the integration network, one with spec.json — while prose grows by judgement. Six changes, each traceable to a finding

Integrations goes top-level

The biggest single change. “Do you support my customers’ systems and the fields I need” is the first question a buyer asks, and today it is answered by a slug table filed under API Reference. Finch scores highest in the field here precisely because Integrations is a top-level section with Provider Network and Field Support inside it. Promoting it forces the gap to be filled.

Integrations becomes a tab, not a group

Today it is one page listing 69 systems. Merge publishes a supported-fields matrix per integration, Finch a filterable field-support tool with CSV export, Kombo roughly 280 connector pages. Filled to that standard this is a 69-page catalog — a tab, not a group. Filing it as a group would have repeated the mistake of under-provisioning the thing already identified as the biggest gap.

Tabs split by growth rate

Guides grow by judgement, one page at a time, and need a rule before each one. The two catalogs grow mechanically — Integrations with the network, API Reference from spec.json, where 136 pages already regenerate with no decisions at all. Keeping them apart means catalogue growth can never dilute the prose.

Ordered by project phase

Evaluate → build → run, which is how integration projects actually go and how Finch sequences its sidebar. v3 ordered topics as a list; this orders them as a path.

Use cases at position two

Stripe opens its documentation with use cases before products. These six pages are the strongest evaluation asset Bindbee has and they sat in group 10 of 10 as recently as this week.

Data models becomes its own subject

v3 piled 18 pages and 15,279 words into “Reading data” because what the data means and how to fetch it were treated as one topic. Split, the fetching mechanics collapse to four pages and the models organise by the API’s own three categories — the same shape as the reference tab, so both halves of the docs agree.

What this does not fix

Nothing here creates SDKs, error codes, a changelog or a field-coverage matrix. Those are the four gaps separating Bindbee from Stripe, Plaid and Finch, and none of them is a navigation problem. This map is worth doing because it makes the coverage gap impossible to ignore — not because moving pages closes it.
