---
title: "Development"
description: "What the Development environment is for, why its connectors never sync on their own, and why you can create as many as you like."
---

Development is where an integration is built and proven before a real customer depends on it. It behaves like Production in every way that matters to your code - same endpoints, same models, same responses - and differs in three ways that matter to how you work.

## What a Development key reaches

Development connectors, their records and their logs. Nothing else. A Development key cannot read Production data, and the reverse is equally true; the key is what enforces the boundary rather than any flag on the request.

That isolation is what makes Development safe to treat casually. A key that can only ever reach sandbox tenants is a key a team can share, keep in a shared `.env`, and paste into a scratch script without a security conversation.

<Warning>
  The same reasoning does not extend to configuration. Scope and custom field
  definitions are organisation-level, so a change made while testing here
  applies to live Production connectors immediately.
</Warning>

## Why syncs never run on their own

Development connectors do not sync on a schedule. Every refresh is one you trigger, from the dashboard or the API.

This is the single most common surprise in the environment, because the symptom is indistinguishable from a bug: a read returns data, the data is simply old, and nothing in the response says so. A connector linked last week and never resynced still answers `200` with last week's employees.

## No connector limit

You can create as many Development connectors as you want, and none of them are billed. Testing against six sandbox tenants costs the same as testing against one.

## Why it works this way

Scheduled extracts are the expensive half of the product - they are real requests against a customer's HR system, subject to that vendor's rate limits and goodwill. Running them unattended for connectors nobody is watching would spend that budget on nothing. Making every Development sync explicit means the cost is only paid when someone actually wants fresh data.

The absent connector limit follows from the same logic in reverse. If a Development connector costs nothing to hold, there is no reason to ration it, and rationing would push teams to test against Production - which is the outcome the environment exists to prevent.

## What this means for you

- **Trigger a sync before trusting Development data**, or you are reading whatever the last manual run produced - see [Sync a Development Connector](/how-to/sync-control/sync-a-development-connector).
- **Never point a production application at a Development key.** It reaches no live data, so the failure is silent rather than loud.
- **Settle scope before you begin testing**, because scope changes reach Production connectors as you make them - see [Restrict Which Models and Fields Sync](/how-to/data-model/restrict-models-and-fields).
- **Use Development for staging.** There is no third environment, and unlimited unbilled connectors make it the practical place to run one.
