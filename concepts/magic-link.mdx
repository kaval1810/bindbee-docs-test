You are not the one who connects to your customer's HR system. They are.

That inversion is easy to skim past, and it shapes a surprising amount - where credentials live, who to contact when a connection breaks, what happens when an employee with admin rights leaves, and why you cannot simply fix a permissions problem yourself.

## Authorisation belongs to the customer

Reaching a company's HR data requires credentials from that company. Only someone with administrative access to their HRIS can grant it, and in almost every case they cannot delegate that to a third party even if they wanted to.

So the flow puts your customer's administrator in the middle. You initiate a connection for a named customer, they authorise it against their own system, and Bindbee holds the resulting credentials. Those credentials never pass through your infrastructure and are never yours to store.

<Info>
This means a connection problem is usually not yours to fix. Expired credentials, insufficient permissions, and revoked access all resolve on the customer's side, and your role is to detect the condition and prompt the right person.
</Info>

## Two ways to present it

Bindbee offers the same authorisation flow through two surfaces.

**Magic Link** is a hosted, in-browser experience delivered as a secure URL. You supply your customer's details, Bindbee generates the link, and you send it to them. They open it, choose their system, and authorise. This requires no front-end code on your side at all, which makes it the right choice for onboarding handled by a human - an implementation manager emailing a new customer, for instance.

**The embedded SDK** puts the same flow inside your own application, so the customer never leaves your product. This is the right choice when connecting an HR system is a step in your own onboarding rather than a separate implementation task.

The distinction is presentation, not capability. Both produce a connector; both put the customer's administrator in control.

## What the end user actually does

The person opening the link is your customer's HR or IT administrator, not a developer. They see their own system, are asked to authorise access, and are done.

What varies underneath is considerable. Some systems use OAuth and the whole thing takes seconds. Others require the administrator to generate an API key inside their HRIS first, or create a dedicated integration user with specific role permissions, or configure a file delivery. For those, the flow guides them but cannot complete the work for them - some of it happens in their HR system, not in Bindbee.

This is why per-system setup instructions exist separately, written for an administrator rather than a developer, and why the harder integrations have a longer tail between "link sent" and "connector active".

## Why it works this way

The alternative - collecting your customers' HRIS credentials yourself and passing them to Bindbee - is simpler to build and considerably worse in every other respect.

It makes you a custodian of credentials that grant access to an entire company's employee records, including compensation and bank details. That is a security liability you have no reason to accept and a compliance burden your customers will eventually ask hard questions about. It also fails on its own terms: OAuth-based systems will not issue credentials to anyone but the authorising administrator, so a credential-collection flow cannot support them at all.

Delegating authorisation means the credential exists only between the customer's system and Bindbee. You get access to normalised data without ever holding the keys to the source, and your customer retains the ability to revoke it without involving you.

The cost is a dependency on someone outside your company completing a task, and a class of failure you can detect but not repair. Building for that - noticing an inactive connector and prompting the right person quickly - is most of what operationalising this well requires.

## What this means for you

- **Connection failures are customer-side.** Detect and prompt; do not expect to fix them yourself.
- **Choose the surface by who is doing the connecting.** Human-led implementation suits the hosted link; self-serve onboarding suits the embedded SDK.
- **Budget time between sending a link and data arriving.** Some systems need administrator work inside the HRIS first.
- **Point administrators at the per-system setup guides**, which are written for them rather than for developers.
