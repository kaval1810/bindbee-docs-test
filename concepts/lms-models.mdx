Learning data answers two questions: what training exists, and who has done it. Seven models cover both, and the split between assignment and achievement is the one that matters.

## Assignment and achievement are separate

**Enrollment** is an assignment. Someone has been given a course, or has started one. It says nothing about whether they finished.

**Completion** is an achievement. Someone finished a course, with a completion date and, where the system records it, a result.

Reading enrolments and treating them as training records is the most common error here, and in a compliance context it is a consequential one. Mandatory training reporting needs completions. An enrolment means the training was assigned, which is precisely what someone out of compliance also has.

<Warning>
An enrolment without a matching completion is the definition of outstanding training. If your product reports compliance, that gap is the report - not the enrolment count.
</Warning>

## The catalogue

**Course** is the unit of learning that people are assigned and complete.

**Content** is material within a course - modules, videos, documents. A course may have several. Where a system does not distinguish them, content may be sparse or absent.

**Category** groups courses in the catalogue, using the customer's own taxonomy.

**Skill** is a competency, associated with courses that develop or assess it. Support varies widely; many LMS platforms have no skill concept at all, and the model will be empty for those integrations.

## LMS users are not HRIS employees

**User** is a learner in the LMS. This is a separate identity from the HRIS employee, even when it is the same person.

The systems are provisioned independently, and the identifiers do not correspond. Joining learning data to employee data means matching on a stable attribute - work email is usually the only reliable one - and accepting that some learners will not match, because contractors, former employees, and external participants often have LMS accounts and no HRIS record.

This is worth designing for explicitly rather than treating unmatched users as errors.

## Read-only

No LMS model accepts writes. Enrolling someone in a course, recording a completion, or creating a course are not operations the unified API performs.

Where a customer needs them, [passthrough](/concepts/passthrough) is the route, with the usual cost of integration-specific code.

## Why it works this way

Keeping enrolment and completion separate follows the domain rather than the convenience of a single model.

Collapsing them - a course record per person with a status field - would work until you needed the interesting cases: a learner who enrolled twice, a completion for a course later removed from the catalogue, an assignment with a due date but no progress. Recurring compliance training is the clearest example, where the same course is completed annually and each completion is a distinct, separately-dated fact. A single record with a status can only hold the most recent one.

Read-only reflects source system reality rather than a deliberate limitation. LMS platforms overwhelmingly treat enrolment as something their own administrators or automation rules perform, and expose narrow write APIs or none. Read coverage is the category's mature capability; write coverage largely does not exist upstream to be unified.

## What this means for you

- **Report on completions, not enrolments.** They answer different questions.
- **Expect enrolment without completion.** That gap is signal, not missing data.
- **Match learners to employees on email**, and design for the unmatched case.
- **Expect skills and content to be thin or absent** on several integrations.
- **Plan for read-only.** Any write need goes through passthrough.
