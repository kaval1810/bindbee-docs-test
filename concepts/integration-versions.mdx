Your customer says they use Workday. Or Rippling, or UKG, or Paychex. That is not enough information to know which integration they need, and choosing the wrong one produces a connection that either fails to authorise or syncs less data than expected.

A vendor is not an integration. Several vendors appear as more than one.

## Why one vendor becomes several integrations

**API version.** A vendor releases a new API generation with different endpoints and different capabilities, and customers migrate at their own pace. Both have to be supported concurrently.

**Product line.** Some vendors sell distinct products under one brand, aimed at different market segments, with genuinely different APIs. UKG Pro and UKG Ready are the clearest case - they are not tiers of one product, they are separate systems.

**Region.** A vendor operating in several markets frequently runs different platforms behind the same name, particularly where local payroll and compliance rules differ enough to require it.

**Edition or add-on.** Access to certain data can depend on which package the customer has bought. A tenant without the relevant module cannot expose that data regardless of permissions.

**Transport.** The same vendor may be reachable by API for some customers and by file delivery for others, depending on what their contract or configuration allows. These behave very differently - see [SFTP & file connectors](/concepts/sftp-and-file-connectors).

**Access method.** Some systems expose more than one route to the same data - a standard API, and a reporting interface with different coverage, latency, and permission requirements.

**Legacy generation.** Following an acquisition or a platform rewrite, the old system persists under the old or a modified name while new customers are onboarded to the new one.

## The identifier is the answer

Each integration has its own slug, and that slug - not the vendor's brand name - is what identifies the system a connector is talking to.

It appears in webhook payloads, in custom field configuration, and on the connector itself. When a customer reports a problem, the slug is the first thing to establish, because behaviour attributed to "Rippling" may be true of one Rippling integration and not the other.

<Info>
This is also why a custom field mapping that works for one customer can fail for another on what appears to be the same system. Organisation-scoped mappings attach to an integration slug. Two customers on different variants of the same vendor are on different integrations, and each needs its own mapping.
</Info>

## Coverage is per integration, not per vendor

Models and fields available are properties of the specific integration. A vendor's newer API may expose benefits data its predecessor does not. A file-based route almost always carries less than an API route.

Statements of the form "we support Vendor X" are therefore imprecise in a way that matters during evaluation. The question a prospect actually needs answered is which variant their tenant runs and what that variant covers.

## Why it works this way

Collapsing variants into a single integration per vendor would be tidier in the catalogue and wrong in operation.

The variants are not cosmetic. UKG Pro and UKG Ready have different data models and different authentication. A vendor's v2 and v3 APIs expose different objects. A file feed cannot answer questions an API can. Presenting these as one integration would mean a connector whose actual capabilities were unknown until it ran, and coverage claims that were true for some customers and false for others with no way to tell which.

Keeping them separate makes capability a property of something specific and checkable. The cost is a catalogue with entries that look confusingly similar, and an onboarding step where someone must establish which variant a customer runs. That step is unavoidable - it exists because the underlying difference is real.

## What this means for you

- **Establish the variant during onboarding**, not after the connector fails.
- **Use the integration slug as the identifier** in your own records and support tooling.
- **Check coverage per integration**, not per vendor, when evaluating whether a customer's requirement is met.
- **Expect organisation-scoped configuration to need duplicating** across variants of the same vendor.
- **Ask which variant** when a customer reports behaviour that contradicts what you know about a system.
